# Writing rules for the Peer Set

Apply these to every sentence you write. The check script enforces the mechanical ones.

## Language
- American spelling: favorable, organization, color, analyze, license.
- Credit-desk register: measured, precise, declarative. State the position and the risk. Understatement
  reads as more credible than emphasis.
- No em dashes. Use commas, periods or semicolons.
- No hype or filler: best-in-class, robust, compelling, well-positioned, world-class, market-leading, poised
  to, "key" as filler, arguably, quite, very. No "significant" without a number. No "strong" without a
  benchmark. No metaphor and no rhetorical questions.
- Spell out every acronym on first use.
- Wrap key figures (amounts, percentages, ratios, multiples, dates) in double asterisks in narrative. Not in
  table cells.
- Quantify wherever a figure exists. Name risks plainly.
- Never expose the system behind the output: no "the vault," "the tile," "the AI summary," "the reads,"
  "the run," prompt numbers, source index numbers or internal identifiers. Name the issuer and filing, or
  "Deal documents."
- Do not state the same fact twice in consecutive sentences, and do not restate in prose what an adjacent
  table shows.

## Sentences
- Lead with the evidence and the figure. Close on what governs the read.
- One idea per sentence. No comma-and-"but" chains.
- No verdict followed by a hedge ("manageable but relevant"). State the finding, then state the constraint as
  its own sentence.
- A finding is a statement, never a question.
- Headings are plain noun phrases. No questions, no colons, no editorial phrases.

## Credit implication
A benchmark finding is only useful once it answers for the borrower. Lead with the borrower's own figure,
set it against the peer evidence, and close on what it means for repayment. Two to four sentences. Where the
borrower's position is genuinely undisclosed, name the gap. Never manufacture a figure.

## Discipline
- No freestanding editorial adjective unless it traces to a stored field.
- Where two sources disagree, report the conflict. Do not resolve it silently, and do not average it.
- A stored section's internal inconsistency counts against that section, not as a tiebreaker.
- Ratings, severities and verdicts are quoted from the stored analysis, never composed.
