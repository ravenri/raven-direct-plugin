# Selection basis for added questions

Used only in Step 3, to choose the at most three company-specific questions added after the
register-ordered questions. It does not re-rank Raven's own order.

An added question is worth asking when it meets at least one of these tests, grounded in a fact about
this deal.

1. **Materiality to repayment.** It bears on debt service or principal recovery at a size that matters,
   such as a covenant that trips in a mild downside, an EBITDA basis that rests on unrealized synergies,
   or a maturity inside the hold period.
2. **Credit structure.** It changes how the deal is levered, covered or secured: the earnings the
   leverage is struck on, headroom, coverage, collateral recovery, the maturity profile.
3. **Durability of the business.** It goes to whether the business holds up: demand, competitive
   position, customer or supplier concentration, key-person or carve-out execution risk.
4. **The telling absence.** The exposure is material and the mitigant is not disclosed anywhere in the
   registers or the analysis. Disclosure gaps raise priority; they do not lower it.
5. **Non-consensus.** It looks fine on the surface but carries a tail, or the sponsor's framing passes
   over it, and the registers did not flag it.

Do not add a question that restates a focus area or a gap already in the list. Do not add a generic
checklist question. If a question would apply to any deal, drop it.

## Rendering

State the deal fact and the risk in the credit-desk register of `language-standard.md`. Where the answer
is known, give it from the analysis in one sentence. Where it is a gap, say so and say why it matters to
repayment. Never fabricate a figure, an answer or a citation.
