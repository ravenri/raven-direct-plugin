# Deal type, analysis subject and entity scope (Raven standard)

Raven's rule block, reproduced as written. Apply it throughout the memo.

---

DEAL TYPE, ANALYSIS SUBJECT AND ENTITY SCOPE

Before reading any figure, know what kind of transaction this is and whose numbers
the credit is against. Getting the entity wrong is the most damaging error
available to you, because the resulting figures are internally consistent and
therefore look correct.

DO NOT DEFAULT TO AN LBO FRAMING
Classify on the primary purpose the documents actually evidence. Assuming a
sponsor buyout where the documents describe a refinancing distorts every
downstream read.

DEAL TYPES — classify into exactly ONE
- REFINANCING — existing debt repaid or replaced; no change of control, no new
  sponsor equity.
- LBO — a financial sponsor is purchasing the company with a purchase price and
  new equity; a new capital structure is put in place.
- ACQUISITION — a strategic or financial acquirer purchases a single target; a
  change of control, but not the classic sponsor-LBO build.
- ADD-ON — an existing platform is acquiring a target, funded through the
  platform's facilities or an incremental raise; the platform remains the
  borrower.
- MERGER-COMBINATION — two operating businesses of comparable substance combine
  into one credit group (platform-plus-target of real weight, or a merger of
  equals); the pro forma is a genuine combination, not a small tuck-in.
- RECAPITALIZATION — new debt raised or capital structure adjusted without a
  change in ownership, often returning cash to shareholders.
- GROWTH FINANCING — new capital primarily to fund organic growth, capex or
  expansion.
- OTHER — state a one-line reason if none fit.

Where the documents show more than one characteristic — an add-on that also
refinances the platform stack, a refinancing paired with a dividend — classify on
the PRIMARY purpose and note the secondary characteristic.

ANALYSIS SUBJECT — single entity or combined group
- SINGLE ENTITY: LBO, Acquisition, Refinancing, Recapitalization, Growth
  Financing, and Add-ons where a platform absorbs a small target (the platform is
  the borrower).
- COMBINED GROUP: Merger-Combination, and material Add-ons where the pro forma
  genuinely combines two operating businesses. Name each constituent and its role
  (platform / acquirer, target / acquired).

Where the subject is a COMBINED GROUP, all downstream financial assembly — the
operative EBITDA, leverage, coverage and capital structure — is struck on the
COMBINED group, never on a single constituent. Do not sum constituents to
construct a combined figure the documents do not present; use the combined
presentation the documents give.

MULTIPLE PERIMETERS ON ONE ENTITY
A single-entity deal can still carry more than one candidate set of financials: a
carve-out perimeter and its parent, an OpCo and a HoldCo, a restricted group and
the consolidated group. This is NOT a combination. Where more than one perimeter
exists for what is one entity, identify each, state which one the credit is
extended to, and name the others explicitly so they are not pulled by mistake.

THE ENTITY SCOPE TEST
Where the deal type is Refinancing, LBO, Recapitalization or Growth Financing, the
documents describe one operating business. Proceed normally; no scope test applies.

Where the deal type is Acquisition, Add-on or Merger-Combination, the documents
will contain figures for more than one entity — the acquirer or platform, the
target, and often a combined pro forma. Select the chart, table or figure matching
the entity the credit is against.

A source covering one entity alone is the WRONG SCOPE where the credit is against
the combined group, EVEN WHERE IT FOOTS TO 100% ON ITS OWN. An internally
consistent figure for the wrong entity is the most common form of this error, and
no percentage or footing check will catch it. Where only single-entity sources
exist, say so plainly and label which entity each covers. Do not blend them, and
do not present one entity's figure as the whole.

Where the deal type is Other, apply the test if the documents carry figures for
more than one entity.

AGREEMENT IS NOT EVIDENCE OF SCOPE
Two analyses that quietly read the same wrong entity will agree with each other
while both being wrong. Consistency between figures is not confirmation that the
perimeter is right — confirm the perimeter against the documents.

LABEL IS NOT ENTITY
A set labeled "Combined", "Consolidated", "Pro Forma" or "Total" may be one
entity's own internal roll-up rather than the deal's credit group. Confirm which
entities a set actually covers before treating it as combined; where the documents
do not make that clear, say so.

PERIMETER DISCONTINUITY
Compare the scale of the disclosed historical series against the first projected
period. Where the two differ by a factor organic growth cannot explain — a step
change rather than a trend — that discontinuity is itself evidence of a perimeter
change, and it is often the only visible evidence. State it: name both figures,
their periods, and the implied change in perimeter. Never attribute a step change
of that kind to growth, and never report the two series as one continuous history.

EXPECTED ABSENCE VERSUS GENUINE GAP
Not every missing statement set is a gap. On an asset purchase, entity-level
statements may not exist at all and asset-level information is what the deal
carries — that is the deal's shape, not an omission. On an add-on, a combined
HISTORICAL set is usually not disclosed; combined figures typically appear only in
a model, forward of close. On a merger-combination, all three sets — each
constituent standalone and the combined group — are expected, and a missing one IS
a genuine gap. Distinguish the two and say which you are reporting.

DESCRIBE, DO NOT ASSUME
Describe only what the documents state. Where something is absent, say "Not
disclosed" rather than inferring it from the deal type. The deal type tells you
what to EXPECT; it never tells you what is true.
