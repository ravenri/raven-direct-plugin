# Canonical consistency and conflict handling (Raven standard)

Raven's rule block, reproduced as written. Apply it throughout the memo.

---

CANONICAL CONSISTENCY

The same question about a deal must get the same answer everywhere it is asked. A
reader who sees one earnings figure on one page and a different one on the next
cannot trust either, even when both are individually defensible.

CONSUME, DO NOT RE-RESOLVE
Certain facts are established once for a deal and then govern everything. Where any
of these has already been established, use it exactly as given and do not
re-derive, recompute or improve on it:
  - the reporting currency, its notation, and the table scale;
  - the canonical anchor period, the historical range, and the forecast horizon;
  - the operative EBITDA figure and its canonical title;
  - the deal type and the entity the credit is extended to;
  - the authoritative source chosen for each major artifact.
Re-resolving a settled fact is how four surfaces come to disagree. If you believe
an established value is wrong, say so explicitly and give your evidence; do not
quietly substitute your own.

WHERE YOU MUST DECIDE
Where a determination has not been made and you have to make it, make it
deliberately and make it legible:
  - state the value you selected;
  - name the source document it came from, and the period it belongs to;
  - state the basis on which you chose that source over the alternatives.
That record is what lets every later reader follow the same choice instead of
starting over. A determination made silently is a determination that will be made
differently next time.

CLASSIFY WHAT YOU ARE ASSERTING
Distinguish three things and never present a later one as an earlier one:
  - STATED — the documents say it, in those terms.
  - DERIVED — you computed it from stated figures, and the computation is
    disclosed.
  - INFERRED — you concluded it from context, and the documents do not state it.
An inferred figure presented as stated is the most expensive error in this system,
because nothing downstream can detect it.

EVERY FIGURE NAMES ITS SOURCE
Attribute each figure to the specific document it came from, with its period.
Where the task provides a catalog of source material, attribute to the specific
entries in that catalog rather than to a document in general. A figure whose origin
cannot be traced is not usable by a credit committee, however plausible it looks.

DO NOT PATCH A PACKAGE SILENTLY
Where you have chosen one source for an artifact and a needed figure is missing
from it, do not fill the gap from another document without saying so. Either state
that this one figure comes from elsewhere and name both sources, or report the gap.
An invisible seam produces a package that no longer foots to itself and gives no
clue why.

WHEN SOURCES GENUINELY DISAGREE
A material disagreement between two plausible sources is not a tie for you to
break. Report both values with their sources and periods, and state plainly that
they diverge. Do not average them, do not silently prefer the higher or the more
recent, and do not drop one.

Where the difference is immaterial — rounding, or a presentational difference that
does not change the credit read — use your chosen source and say nothing. Judge
materiality by whether the difference would change a conclusion, not by size alone.

CONSISTENCY IS NOT PROOF
Two figures agreeing with each other is not evidence that either is right. They
may agree because both read the same wrong source, the same wrong period, or the
same wrong entity. Verify against the documents, not against each other.

FLAG RATHER THAN SMOOTH
Where the evidence is genuinely incomplete, contradictory, or points somewhere
uncomfortable, say so. An honest gap is recoverable by the next reader. A gap
filled with something plausible is not.
