# EBITDA definitions and basis discipline (Raven standard)

Raven's rule block, reproduced as written. Apply it throughout the memo.

---

EBITDA DEFINITIONS AND BASIS DISCIPLINE

Deals disclose several EBITDA figures under several names. Reporting the wrong one,
or reporting one without naming it, is how the same company ends up with different
earnings on different pages. Use the definitions below, and name the figure every
time you use it.

THE DOCUMENTS DEFINE THE CONTENT
Use the labels the documents use, and let the documents determine what falls into
each figure. Do not impose an independent definition of EBITDA, and never infer or
construct a figure the documents do not state. The role descriptions below
describe TYPICAL content so you can map a label to a role — they are not a
definition to enforce against the documents.

THE FOUR CANONICAL ROLES
- REPORTED EBITDA — EBITDA per the financial statements before adjustments; the
  unadjusted starting point.
- ADJUSTED EBITDA — the borrower's primary adjusted figure, and the one
  predominant in credit analysis. Typically adds back non-cash items (stock-based
  compensation, impairments), non-recurring or one-time costs (restructuring,
  transaction and deal costs, litigation) and normalizing items (owner
  compensation, discontinued lines) — that is, adjustments to results ALREADY
  EARNED. Idiosyncratic labels such as "Sponsor Adjusted" or "Management
  Adjusted" map here when they describe the borrower's primary adjusted figure.
- PRO FORMA ADJUSTED EBITDA — the further-adjusted figure, present only where the
  documents disclose it SEPARATELY from Adjusted EBITDA. Typically layers
  forward-looking or transaction-driven items on top: run-rate cost savings,
  synergies, the annualized effect of acquisitions or price increases, and other
  not-yet-realized adjustments — that is, items reflecting a FUTURE or pro-forma
  state rather than results already earned. Where the documents disclose only one
  adjusted figure, there is no separate pro forma layer; do not invent one.
- PF ADJUSTED CASH EBITDA (software and recurring-revenue deals only) — Pro Forma
  Adjusted EBITDA further adjusted for the period change in short-term deferred
  revenue, converting the figure from an accrual basis to a cash-collections
  basis. A decrease in deferred revenue is subtractive, an increase additive.
  Where the current / non-current split is not disclosed, use total deferred
  revenue. Do NOT apply it where the source's adjusted figure already includes the
  deferred-revenue movement — in that case state that it is already included. It
  sits one line below Pro Forma Adjusted EBITDA in the walk.

THE OPERATIVE (ANCHOR) FIGURE
The operative figure for the deal is the MOST-ADJUSTED EBITDA the documents
disclose: Pro Forma Adjusted EBITDA, or Adjusted EBITDA where there is no separate
pro forma figure. For software and recurring-revenue deals where the documents
present the deferred-revenue adjustment, it is PF Adjusted Cash EBITDA.

Where the credit is against a combined group rather than a single company, the
operative figure is the combined-group figure the combined presentation gives.
Select it the same way — do not build a combined figure by summing constituents.

If an operative figure has already been established for this deal, use it and do
not re-select. Otherwise select it by the rule above and hold it for everything you
produce.

NAME IT BY ITS CANONICAL TITLE
When you report the operative figure, call it by its canonical title — Reported
EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, PF Adjusted EBITDA, or PF Adjusted Cash EBITDA. Never
write "anchor EBITDA", "operative EBITDA" or any internal shorthand in output a
reader sees; those are role names for reasoning, not labels for the page.

THE LEVERAGE BASIS RULE
Every leverage, coverage and attachment multiple strikes on the operative figure,
and NAMES the EBITDA it is struck against. A multiple without its basis is not a
usable fact: 4.4x against Reported and 4.4x against PF Adjusted are different
statements about the credit. Where two related figures across different sections
appear to contradict, the cause is usually an unnamed basis rather than an error.

Leverage multiples strike on DRAWN debt, not committed. Undrawn facilities are
excluded from the multiples; state committed and drawn separately where both are
disclosed.

SOFTWARE DEALS — KEEP THE ACCRUAL FIGURE VISIBLE
Where the operative figure is the deferred-revenue-adjusted one, always disclose
alongside it both the deferred-revenue adjustment amount and the accrual pro forma
or adjusted figure, stated directionally — for example "absent the
deferred-revenue adjustment, EBITDA would be $X and leverage lower". The accrual
figure must never disappear behind the cash figure.

MAPPING IDIOSYNCRATIC LABELS
Where a label is idiosyncratic ("Financing EBITDA", "Financeable EBITDA",
"Run-Rate EBITDA"), map it to the closest of the four roles and state the reasoning
in one sentence. Where two or more differently-labelled figures map to the SAME
role — "Management Adjusted" and "Sponsor Adjusted" both being the primary
adjusted figure — keep them as separate entries with their own values and sources
rather than merging them, and state which one governs. A disclosed EBITDA that
corresponds to none of the four roles (a segment EBITDA, a covenant-specific
EBITDA) is not one of these figures and need not be carried, unless it is an alias
of one of them.

ADD-BACKS AND SYNERGIES — LABEL THE STATUS
Every add-back and synergy figure names its status: identified, included in the
adjusted or pro forma figure, or targeted run-rate. Where the operative figure
includes cost saves or synergies, state:
  - the dollar amount;
  - the label the documents use — cost saves, synergies, run-rate savings,
    operational improvements — in the document's exact term;
  - the validation status: sponsor-identified only, third-party validated, or
    agreed with management, naming the validating source.
Where it includes none, say so explicitly.

Status labeling is for clarity, not for surfacing a discrepancy. Two figures that
differ only because one includes synergies and the other does not are not in
conflict once both are labeled.

THE WALK MUST BE HONEST, NOT TIDY
A reconciliation walks Reported to Adjusted to Pro Forma Adjusted, in that order:
base, then typed add-backs and deductions, then a subtotal, then synergies, then
the total. Deductions carry negative values. Where the documents disclose no pro
forma layer, the walk ends at the subtotal — no synergy rows, no total row.

Where a walk's total legitimately differs from the operative figure, do NOT force
it to reconcile. State both and note the difference. The walk establishes earnings
quality; the operative figure is what the multiples strike on. They may
legitimately diverge, and a forced reconciliation hides exactly the fact a credit
committee needs.

WHEN SOURCES DISCLOSE DIFFERENT VALUES
Where two sources disclose a differing value for the same role in the same period
beyond a trivial rounding difference, report both with their sources and state that
they diverge. Do not average them, and do not silently prefer one.
