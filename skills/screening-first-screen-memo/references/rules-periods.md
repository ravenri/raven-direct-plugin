# Period conventions (Raven standard)

Raven's rule block, reproduced as written. Apply it throughout the memo.

---

PERIOD CONVENTIONS

Every deal has ONE canonical anchor period and ONE historical range. Every
headline figure, and every leverage, coverage and attachment multiple, is struck
on the anchor. If an anchor has already been established for this deal, use it
exactly and do not revisit the choice. Otherwise determine it by the rules below,
and then hold it for everything you produce.

PERIOD VOCABULARY
Every period has a TYPE — q1, q2, q3, fiscal_year, or ltm — and a BASIS: actual,
budget, forecast, or estimate. A period may additionally be PRO FORMA, which is
orthogonal to type: an LTM presented pro forma for an acquisition is type=ltm,
pro_forma=Yes. Where a period is pro forma, describe the adjustment behind it —
acquisition, synergies, divestiture, or other. Use these terms exactly.

A period is a distinct point in time, identified by its END DATE. "2023 A" and
"FY 2023" are the SAME period when they share an end date — carry it once, noting
both labels, never as two columns.

THE ACTUAL PERIODS BOUNDARY
The most recent fiscal year with disclosed actuals (e.g. FY2025A). This bounds the
historical range. It is NOT necessarily the anchor — a deal can have actuals
through FY2025 and still be anchored on an LTM or a current-year expected figure.
Establish it separately and do not conflate the two.

SELECTING THE CANONICAL ANCHOR
1. Determine the fiscal year in progress as of the analysis date, using the
   company's OWN fiscal year-end — not the calendar year. Any period that is not
   a fiscal_year or ltm period is ineligible for the anchor position; identify
   those so they are not used.
2. If the documents disclose a TTM/LTM period ending within that fiscal year, the
   anchor is that period. Use it as disclosed; do not calculate it. Where more
   than one such TTM/LTM exists, the anchor is the MOST RECENT one, in whichever
   document discloses it — including a later addendum or supplement.
3. If no TTM/LTM ends within the current fiscal year, the anchor is the full-year
   expected figure for the current fiscal year (e.g. FY2026E).
4. Never anchor on a q1 / q2 / q3 period. Never construct a period the governing
   source does not state.
5. Where the credit is against a combined group rather than a single company, the
   anchor is the combined-group period the combined presentation itself gives.
   Do not assemble one from constituents.
Name the anchor, its end date, its type, whether it is actual or projected, and
the document that discloses it.

THE HISTORICAL RANGE
The historical range is the full span of disclosed ACTUAL fiscal years, earliest
through the most recent fiscal year with actuals. Do not drop an interior year for
being volatile, anomalous, or inconvenient. Where a year is missing one metric,
keep the year and note the gap in that metric.

THE FORECAST HORIZON
The projection range as disclosed. Where no projection exists, say so rather than
extending history forward.

TYPE ELIGIBILITY — the rule violated most often
Annual analysis is drawn ONLY from `ltm` and `fiscal_year` periods. A q1 / q2 /
q3 period may NEVER appear in:
  - the historical financials table or any annual series
  - the EBITDA reconciliation or bridge
  - any leverage, coverage or attachment multiple
  - the canonical anchor position
Quarterly periods ARE the correct basis for intra-year work: seasonality,
working-capital swings, liquidity troughs, covenant testing dates, and
current-period performance. Where you use them, label them explicitly as quarters
and never mix them into an annual series.

STUB PERIODS
A partial-period figure is not comparable in magnitude to a full period. Where a
stub appears beside full periods, flag it as a stub and treat any level
comparison as directional only. Never present a stub as if it were a full quarter
or year.

NEVER CONSTRUCT A PERIOD
Report only periods a source states. Do not build an LTM by summing quarters, do
not annualize a partial period, and do not interpolate a missing year — even when
the arithmetic looks obvious. Once rendered, a constructed period is
indistinguishable from a disclosed one, which is what makes it dangerous.

LABELING
Use the period label as the source discloses it, verbatim. Do not restyle
"LTM 6/30/24" into "TTM Jun-24", or "FY 2023" into "2023A". In any annual series
at most ONE LTM-style label may appear, and only in the final position; earlier
interim or LTM disclosures belong in current-period analysis, not the annual
series.

ORDERING
Chronological, always: oldest on the left, most recent on the right. Tables,
series and chart axes alike.

ACTUAL VERSUS PROJECTED
Where a series mixes them, the boundary must be visible — mark which periods are
actual and which are projected. A reader must never have to infer where history
ends and forecast begins.

MISALIGNED PERIOD SETS
Some statements legitimately render on their own period set — a balance sheet on
fiscal year-ends plus an interim, a cash flow statement on a shorter window.
Where a set cannot align to the annual periods, render it on its own disclosed
periods and note the difference ONCE. Do not force-fit it, and do not pad it with
nulls to match a series it does not belong to.

COMPARISON DISCIPLINE
Compare like with like. A year-over-year read pairs a period with the SAME period
type one year earlier — never a stub against a full prior-year quarter, and never
an LTM against a fiscal year without saying so. State the comparison basis
wherever the pairing is not self-evident.

EVERY FIGURE CARRIES ITS PERIOD
State the period any figure belongs to. Every multiple names the period it is
struck on. A figure without a period is not a fact a credit committee can use.
